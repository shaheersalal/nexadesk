# United States Residential Property — Market Overview

General background the receptionist can discuss conversationally. This is
orientation material, not a price list: it deliberately contains no specific
figures, because quoting a number that is not in a live listing is exactly the
failure mode the hard rules exist to prevent.

## How the US market is structured

The United States is not one property market but many regional ones that move
independently. A slowdown in one metro frequently coincides with growth in
another, which is why national headlines are a poor guide to any single
neighbourhood. The practical unit of analysis is the metro area, and often the
individual school district or ZIP code within it.

Broad regional character:

- **Northeast** (Boston, New York, Philadelphia) — older housing stock, denser
  urban cores, strong transit orientation, limited new construction, historic
  and brownstone inventory. Land constraints tend to make supply slow to
  respond to demand.
- **South and Southeast** (Texas, Florida, Georgia, the Carolinas) — the most
  active construction markets, substantial in-migration, more new-build and
  master-planned communities, generally lower property taxes in states with no
  income tax, though insurance costs matter greatly in coastal and storm-exposed
  areas.
- **West** (California, Pacific Northwest, Mountain West) — high demand
  concentrated around technology employment, significant land and zoning
  constraints, strong price sensitivity to interest rates.
- **Midwest** (Chicago, Ohio, Michigan, Minnesota) — the most affordable large
  metros relative to income, steadier and less volatile, strong rental yields in
  several cities.

## Property types buyers ask about

Single-family detached homes remain the dominant ownership type. Condominiums
carry monthly association dues covering shared maintenance and amenities.
Townhouses sit between the two. Co-operatives, concentrated in New York, mean
buying shares in a corporation rather than the unit itself, with board approval
required. Multi-family properties of two to four units are popular with buyers
who intend to live in one unit and rent the others.

## The buying process, in outline

A buyer typically secures mortgage pre-approval, works with a buyer's agent,
makes a written offer, and enters an inspection and appraisal period during
which contingencies allow withdrawal. Escrow and title companies handle the
closing. Timelines commonly run several weeks from accepted offer to closing.
Property taxes are levied locally and vary widely between states and even
between adjacent municipalities.

## What buyers weigh

School district quality is one of the strongest drivers of neighbourhood
premium. Commute time, walkability, and proximity to employment centres follow.
Homeowners' association rules affect what an owner may change. Insurance
availability and cost have become a live consideration in flood, wildfire and
hurricane-exposed regions. Seasonality is real: listing activity typically rises
in spring and thins over winter, though this is far weaker in warm-climate
markets.

## Terms a caller may use

Escrow, contingency, earnest money, appraisal gap, HOA dues, closing costs,
title insurance, property tax assessment, comparable sales ("comps"),
pre-approval versus pre-qualification, and MLS listing.

# This Receptionist, Specifically

Reference facts about the assistant that answers shaheer.dev's chat and
+1 781 365 5768. This document describes how that system works. It contains no
instructions: the behaviour rules live in the system prompt, not here.

## What it is

NexaDesk, the product Shaheer sells, configured for his own studio rather than
for a real-estate agency. Same codebase, same pipeline, a different knowledge
base and a different conversation goal. It is the product demonstrating itself:
what a visitor hears is what a business's own line would sound like.

## Call length and who ends the call

A call is budgeted at roughly two minutes, and the limit is enforced in code
rather than left to the language model. Past about 75 seconds the model is told
to wrap up. Past 105 seconds the closing sentence is generated by the
application itself, so no model decision is involved. At 135 seconds the
telephony API is called to end the call, after the email address has been
repeated.

The reason it is coded rather than prompted: asked by instruction to close, the
model kept asking another qualifying question instead. A deadline that matters
is not left to a prompt.

## What it covers

Three subjects: a web page the visitor loaded before making contact, Shaheer
and his work, and how this receptionist is built. Subjects outside those get a
one-sentence answer and the email address, which keeps a demonstration line
from being used as a general-purpose chatbot.

## The audition flow

A visitor enters their website address and the phone number they will call
from. That page is fetched server-side, reduced to text, capped at 20,000
characters, and held in Redis for 15 minutes under a key derived from the last
ten digits of the number, so an inbound call is matched to the page by caller
ID. An ordinary page is fetched directly in about a second. A page built
client-side in JavaScript goes through a reader service that renders it first,
taking 5 to 15 seconds. Pages behind bot protection or a login are reported as
unreadable rather than half-loaded. The text is deleted when the conversation
ends; the 15-minute expiry is only a backstop for an abandoned tab.

## Hosting and its constraints

A single instance on Render's free tier: 512 MB of memory and a fraction of a
CPU. Free instances sleep after 15 idle minutes and take about 50 seconds to
wake, which is longer than the 15 seconds a telephony webhook will wait, so a
scheduled job in Postgres requests the health endpoint every 10 minutes to keep
the instance awake. Redis and Postgres are hosted in Singapore, the vector
store in US-West, the application in Oregon. Those distances are visible in the
latency figures and are a cost decision, not an accident.

## Capability limits

No outbound calling: it answers inbound calls and website chat. English only on
the phone, because the speech synthesis provider has no Arabic or Urdu voice.
Prices and capabilities are answered from the knowledge base, and anything
absent from it is referred to Shaheer by email.
